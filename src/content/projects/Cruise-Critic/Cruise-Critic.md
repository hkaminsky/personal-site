In June 2023 at Work & Co, we partnered with **Cruise Critic (a Tripadvisor company)** to rethink and redesign their site with a clear goal: **increase engagement and shopping intent—especially for new audiences—while setting Cruise Critic up for measurable conversion and growth over time.**

Because the engagement was shorter than a typical full build, our focus was to define a strong strategic direction and deliver a practical, testable design foundation Cruise Critic could roll out through A/B testing.

## Outcomes we were driving toward

We designed against three business outcomes, each with explicit success metrics:

1) **Higher shopping intent and cruise bookings**  
Measured by: percentage of shoppers moving through stages of the shopping funnel, and web booking conversion rate

2) **Higher engagement and repeat usage**  
Measured by: 7-day repeat visit rate, clicks per shopper, and revenue per click

3) **More free traffic via improved SEO and increased brand affinity**  
Measured by: organic traffic (free/paid), search engine rank, and direct visits

## Priority audiences

We grounded the work in three core audiences with different needs and mindsets:

- **First-time cruisers**
- **Casual cruisers**
- **Avid cruisers**

## How we approached the work

We began by building shared understanding of the business and current experience, then moved quickly into research and synthesis:

- Reviewed business context and the current-state user experience  
- Led a competitive and landscape analysis (in-category and adjacent)  
- Interviewed stakeholders, current users, and prospective users  
- Developed personas and jobs-to-be-done  
- Synthesized findings into a product brief with strategic imperatives to guide concepting

That product brief became the anchor: a clear articulation of what the experience needed to prioritize based on both user needs and Cruise Critic’s business reality.

## The big constraint

A major challenge was Cruise Critic’s **ad-driven revenue model**. It couldn’t be disrupted—ads were a primary revenue source—so our approach needed to be **additive**: protect existing revenue while exploring new opportunities.

## Key challenges (and the opportunities they created)

Our research distilled into five core challenges that shaped the design direction:

1. **Planning a cruise feels like work** → Make it exciting & inspirational  
2. **Newer cruisers need handholding** → Educate and guide them  
3. **They’re overwhelmed by choice** → Curate options  
4. **They overlook your expertise** → Deliver it in the right moments  
5. **They want simplicity & transparency** → Simplify the booking process  

## What we delivered

Given the project timeline and the need to minimize disruption, we focused on three core user journeys and created a suite of templates + a component library that Cruise Critic could use as a scalable foundation for iterative testing.

![Cruise Critic Redesign](/project-images/cruise-critic-images/cruise-detail-page.png)

### Core journeys we designed for

- Discovering Cruise Critic through Google  
- Learning about options while researching cruises  
- Returning to Cruise Critic after having already gone on a cruise

![Cruise Critic Redesign](/project-images/cruise-critic-images/cruise-wizard.png)

### Deliverables

- End-to-end designs across the three journeys  
- A set of reusable page templates  
- A component library designed to support gradual rollout and A/B testing

## What I’m proud of

This work balanced real constraints with real ambition: creating a path to higher intent, engagement, and SEO improvements **without disrupting the revenue engine** that kept the business running. We left Cruise Critic with a strategic north star and a practical toolkit to evolve the experience safely, measurably, and over time.
