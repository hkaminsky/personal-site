In March 2023 at Work & Co, we partnered with **AccuWeather for Business (AFB)**—AccuWeather’s B2B offering that delivers enterprise-grade weather data services through a distributed set of products and services.

AccuWeather’s core business has historically been driven by its D2C, ad-supported model. With ad models becoming more volatile and disrupted, AFB needed a sustainable growth path—and they came to us with a bold ambition: **5x growth** for the B2B business. The team recognized the **client portal** as the key strategic asset to unlock that growth: acquire new customers, retain and expand existing relationships, and create a model that could truly scale.

## The challenge

AFB had deep expertise and powerful capabilities, but the product ecosystem had become complex—hard to sell, hard to onboard, and hard to scale without heavy internal effort. In a growth moment, complexity becomes the bottleneck.

The portal needed to evolve into something that could:
- Clearly communicate value to new customers
- Support self-serve adoption and expansion
- Simplify packaging and delivery
- Modernize the product experience so it feels like a command center, not a utility dashboard

## How we approached it

We started with an intensive discovery and strategy phase to understand both the market and the internal realities:

- Spoke with past, current, and prospective customers across a dozen industries and roles
- Reviewed existing product and business data and the current portal experience
- Completed a competitive analysis
- Interviewed internal stakeholders across product, engineering, sales, marketing, meteorology, operations, business leads, and executive leadership

From there, we synthesized what we heard into core customer groups, clear priorities, and a product strategy grounded in what would actually move the business forward.

## The value proposition we defined

We clarified the portal’s role and value in one line:

**“Weather intelligence you can put to work to save lives & protect assets. A personalized weather command center that turns alerts into action plans.”**

That framing shaped everything—especially the emphasis on turning information into decisions and action.

![AccuWeather for Business Portal](/project-images/accuweather-images/afb-notifications-dashboard.png)

## Strategy: simplify to scale

A key insight was that **reducing complexity** was the unlock for scalability. If every customer requires heavy customization and manual support, growth is capped.

We used tiering as the mechanism to reduce internal strain while increasing clarity for customers: tiers help define what’s included, what’s supported, and what evolves over time.

## What we delivered

### A three-tiered packaging + pricing model

We established a three-tier model with defined feature sets and support levels across tiers, and paired it with a roadmap that laid out how the offerings would evolve from an MVP launch through iterative releases.

### A portal experience directly informed by the strategy

We worked extremely closely with the AFB team and customers to define and design a portal that could become that “weather command center.” Our team essentially embedded with AccuWeather for Business to understand the underlying product complexity, data models, and dependencies—so we could de-complexify the system and design something scalable.

We designed four core areas:

**Home**  
A dashboard for local weather, forecasts, watchlists, settings, and clear pathways into the experience—solving:  
“I want to check in on local weather and quickly see what I need to pay attention to today.”

**Notifications**  
Action-oriented notifications and alerts, with a fully navigable/searchable map and timing of weather events (Gantt-style view) relevant to customer locations—solving:  
“I want to see the most important notifications and events affecting my locations and easily drill into timing and details.”

![AccuWeather for Business Portal](/project-images/accuweather-images/afb-notification-detail.png)

**Locations**  
A structured way to view and manage watchlists and locations, with quick access to location detail panels—solving:  
“I want to see the locations most important to me and easily access the rest.”

![AccuWeather for Business Portal](/project-images/accuweather-images/afb-locations.png)

**Insights**  
A dedicated space to configure reporting on past and future weather events and set alerts for upcoming ones—solving:  
“I want to use weather data, past and upcoming, to make the best decisions for my company.”

![AccuWeather for Business Portal](/project-images/accuweather-images/afb-insights.png)

## What I’m proud of (and the bittersweet ending)

This project was about building more than a redesign—it was about setting up a B2B business to scale through clearer packaging, self-service, and a differentiated product vision.

Midway through development, major leadership and strategic shifts at AFB changed priorities, and the work unfortunately stopped before launch. It’s bittersweet—but looking back, we had the foundation for something that genuinely could have reshaped how enterprise teams operationalize forecasting: not as data, but as coordinated action.
