In March 2022, at Work & Co, I led strategy, design, and development for Traeger’s completely revamped mobile app—native on both iOS and Android.

Our north star value proposition was: **“Endless ways to extend your Traeger experience and reach new heights of flavor.”**

And we aligned the work around three clear goals:

1. **Broaden the base of loyal customers** (make success feel accessible to more people, more often)  
2. **Be perceived as a culinary authority** (teach techniques, not just sell products)  
3. **Develop recurring revenue offerings** (create an ecosystem people return to—not just when they buy a grill)

## The challenge

Traeger isn’t a typical “content app” or a typical “device controller.” It’s a connected cooking companion—meaning the product has to earn trust in high-stakes moments (your cook), work reliably in real environments (backyards, spotty WiFi, distractions), and serve a wide range of users from first-timers to experts.

## What I led

### 1) A unified experience across control + content
We reimagined the app as the place users go to *extend their Traeger experience*—run a cook, learn techniques, discover recipes, and review results—without the experience feeling fragmented.

This foundation supported all three goals: it made the product feel approachable (loyalty), credible (authority), and return-worthy (recurring engagement).



### 2) Connected grill control from anywhere (WiFIRE®)
A core pillar was letting users connect the app to their WiFi-enabled grill so they can monitor and control it remotely—adjust temps, set timers, and get alerts while staying present with friends/family (instead of hovering over the grill).

Reliability and confidence here were essential for broadening loyalty: if the control experience isn’t trustworthy, nothing else matters.

![Traeger App](/project-images/traeger-images/2-connected_grill_controls.png)

### 3) Interactive recipes + guides + videos (culinary authority)
We built a robust library of interactive recipes and educational content—guides and videos designed to teach skills and techniques, not just provide inspiration.

This was a direct expression of the “culinary authority” goal: helping users learn *why* something works, not just *what* to do.

![Traeger App](/project-images/traeger-images/3-interactive_recipes_and_guides.png)

### 4) Guided Cooks — “set it and forget it” (with confidence)
We introduced Guided Cooks: a step-by-step cooking path that lets the user hand off parts of the cook to the grill—timers, temperature changes, and alerts—based on live temperature data.

It reduces anxiety for new cooks (broadening loyalty), reinforces Traeger’s expertise through coaching (authority), and creates an experience you want to repeat (recurring engagement).

![Traeger App](/project-images/traeger-images/4-guided_cooks.png)

### 5) Live + past cook data (make every cook a little better)
We enabled users to track and interact with cook data during and after sessions, building a loop where past cooks help improve future cooks—making progress visible and reinforcing loyalty over time.

![Traeger App](/project-images/traeger-images/5-live_and_past_cook_data.png)

### 6) Photos + sharing
Users could capture and share photos of what they made—bringing pride, personality, and a lightweight social layer into the experience.

### 7) Achievements + badging
We built a branded achievements/badging system to add motivation and stickiness—rewarding exploration, consistency, and skill-building over time, and giving users more reasons to return beyond a single cook.

![Traeger App](/project-images/traeger-images/7-achievements_and_badging.png)

### 8) MEATER integration mid-project
Midway through the work, Traeger acquired MEATER. We had to integrate MEATER probe firmware into the app experience—absorbing new complexity and shifting requirements while maintaining momentum toward launch.

## My role
